Origins

How Pothos came to be, and how it's designed to become excellent

Background - Living in the era of Enshittification

After too many frustrating encounters with SaaS (Software as a Service) providers, we became increasingly frustrated by bait and switch tactics, deceptive subscription models and an overall feeling of big platforms simply providing less and less value relative to their cost. This is not the direction we want the world to go in, and neither is it an excusable one, so we decided to implement a completely new business model. One that attempts to systemise self-improvement at a fundamental level. We saw a lot of value in the open source approach (AOSP, Linux, Git, Blender, FreeCAD etc) and figured that it lines up well with the philosophical outlook of the company.

Enter Pothos

Pothos was developed as a purely internal tool initially to make it as easy as possible to start and operate a business. It was never specifically intended to compete with any of the 'big platforms', but rather to be simple, reliable and extremely functional. We wanted to get Pothos to a point where it is of such high quality that supporting it can generate enough revenue to feed back into its own development. We designed the business model to become a self-sustaining feedback loop of improvements. This way it becomes a type of acceleration multiplier for any business depending on it.

How does an Open Source project make money?

The entire product is completely free and open source without any limitations, so revenue is generated by providing direct business services support, setup, integrations, Service Level Agreements and cloud hosting. Any revenue remaining after covering overhead costs is allocated to system improvements based on the roadmap. This itself is determined primarily by user feedback. This means that each fix, feature, enhancement or expansion is a data driven decision determined by the people using it. It's essentially a free-market mechanic to choose where to spend time and resources and should effectively be the most efficient way to doing so. The ultimate business goal for us is not profit driven but rather to create a system that provides the most value possible to as many businesses as possible.

What's different about this approach?

Allocating revenue to development is how almost all software is made. We have decided to approach this slightly differently. Resources are allocated primarily based on the task scope and demand (rather than fixed developer salaries). So each roadmap item has a monetary value attached to it. Any developer or capable community member can submit code which fulfills the requirement of the task and (provided it passes all security requirements, quality checks and tests) will receive the amount allocated to that task. This democratises the development process, invites codebase scrutiny and actively rewards community involvement, thus improving the system for everyone.

Long term Commitment and Stability

Due to the nature of this system, Pothos ERP is used to manage the Pothos business internally. We are customer zero, meaning we face any failure modes or issues ourselves long before they ever make it to a release. An additional benefit of the system being open source is that it will always be available for use, irrespective of the state of the underlying business. There is no risk of vendor lock-in, no risk of terms changes, no risk of pricing changes, no limit on features and no risk of interrupting your business operations. It is essentially a guarantee of long term stability and consistency, which is exactly what you need if your business depends on it.
In fact, instead of trying to create a walled ecosystem, it is designed such that any integrations are as easy as possible in order to maximise the usefulness to the end user.

Lastly... A.I

When it comes to A.I, there are no built-in A.I chatbots or similar common A.I features. We have always found this approach to be a rather inefficient application of an otherwise extremely useful technology. Instead Pothos makes an MCP server and local LLMWiki available, with fully configurable permissions and access levels, in order to support any A.I model of your choosing (like Claude, ChatGPT, DeepSeek etc). This allows you to run advanced frontier models (or even fully offline ones) with the necessary context and tools natively, thus allowing you to leverage much more use out of the technology and automate to a much greater level than would otherwise be possible. Best of all is that this is completely optional, you can replace the model, and if you decide to use none of it, then it will never get in your way.
